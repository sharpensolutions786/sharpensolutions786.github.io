Single-property websites: why agents are moving listings off portals

A single-property website is exactly what it sounds like — one web page dedicated to one listing, with its own photos, price, amenities, a brochure, and the agent's own profile. Instead of posting a property into a shared portal alongside dozens of competitors, the agent gets a clean link they can put in an ad, a bio, or a WhatsApp message.

The point is owning the lead

On a portal, an enquiry usually lands in a shared pool and may be sold on to several agents. A single-property page flips that: the enquiry goes straight to the agent who owns the page. Tools like My Property Pages send every enquiry directly to the agent's WhatsApp, so the lead is theirs from the first message, not a contact resold five times.

No MLS or IDX feed required

Most markets outside North America have no MLS to plug into, which is why portal-style tools don't fit. With a single-property website you add the property yourself — or paste a portal link and let an importer fill the form — and the page is live in minutes. There is no coding and no portal account to maintain.

Pricing per account, not per listing

The quiet detail that matters is how these are priced. Many builders charge per listing per month, which punishes an active agent. The more sensible model is to price per account and cap by the number of properties, with a free tier to start — the cost of hosting one more page is close to nothing, so charging a per-listing premium is a pricing decision, not a real cost.

A practical note for agents comparing options: check whether leads are delivered to you directly or pooled, whether your licence number can be displayed for compliance, and whether the page is mobile-first (most buyers will open it on a phone).
